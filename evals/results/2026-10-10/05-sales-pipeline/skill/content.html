Bookings missed target three months running, and the gap widened each month

Closed-won was within a few points of target in every quarter until this one: 102% in Q4 2025, 103% in Q1, 99% in Q2, then 91% in Q3. September, at 88% of target, was the weakest month of the last twelve.

The shortfall is not a shortage of pipeline. New pipeline created in Q3 was €18.2M, 7% more than in Q2, and September's €6.7M was the highest month on record in this export. More went in and less came out.

Two yardsticks

Rep quotas for Q3 add up to €4.60M. The bookings target was €5.10M. Reps as a group closed exactly 100% of quota while the company closed 91% of target.

The leak is at the proposal stage, and it is new this quarter

Of 268 deals that reached proposal in Q3, 102 moved on to negotiation. 131 were lost and 35 have sat for more than 60 days. The proposals lost were worth €6.1M, more than the €4.6M won in the whole quarter.

Lead disqualification is the widest ribbon below, but it is not the problem: 41% of leads qualified, the same as in every recent quarter.

Three of the four conversion rates have moved by no more than two points in a year. Proposal to negotiation fell 14 points in one quarter.

What the drop is worth

At Q2's 52%, 139 proposals would have reached negotiation, 37 more than did. At Q3's 70% close rate and €65k average won deal, that is about 26 wins and €1.7M. An estimate: not all would have closed inside the quarter.

Price and silence account for two-thirds of the proposals we lost

47 of the 131 lost proposals were priced above the buyer's approved budget, and 38 ended with no decision. Add the 35 deals stalled for more than 60 days and 73 proposals simply stopped moving, more than were lost on price.

Losses to Corvid (21) and security reviews that did not finish in time (14) are smaller, but both are specific and addressable.

What we cannot say yet

Loss reasons were exported for Q3 only. We cannot show which reason grew when conversion fell; that needs the same export for Q2.

Two EMEA reps closed 45% of the quarter, and Q4 pipeline sits where win rates are lowest

L. Brandt and A. Okafor closed €2.08M between them, at 244% and 156% of quota. EMEA as a whole delivered 58% of closed-won from 43% of the quota. Without those two reps, the other seven closed 71% of their combined quota.

Open pipeline is concentrated the other way. North America's three reps hold €4.6M of the €10.7M open, the three largest books on the team, with win rates of 15% to 25% and the longest sales cycles, 59 to 71 days. Weighted by each rep's Q3 win rate, North America's 43% of open pipeline is closer to 30% of what is likely to close.

What to fix in Q4: the proposal stage first, then where the pipeline sits

Priority

Get proposal-to-negotiation back towards 52%

It is the only stage that moved, and it moved 14 points. Recovering it is worth roughly 37 more deals in negotiation a quarter, several times the €452k Q3 shortfall. The three actions below are proposals for discussion in the review.

Confirm budget before the proposal

47 proposals were priced above an approved budget the buyer already had. Make a confirmed budget figure a condition of moving a deal to proposal, and route exceptions through pricing approval.

Work the deals that went quiet

35 proposals worth €1.7M are stalled past 60 days and still open. Review each in October: re-engage or close out. Then inspect every proposal with no buyer activity at 30 days.

Inspect North America's €4.6M

The largest books carry the lowest win rates. Go through open pipeline deal by deal with the regional manager before it is counted in the Q4 forecast.

To reduce reliance on two reps, find out what Brandt and Okafor do differently at proposal (they win 47–48% of deals in 36–41 days) and apply it elsewhere. Rep quotas and the bookings target should also be aligned: they differ by €500k, so the team can hit quota and the company still miss.

Two smaller items: start security reviews before the proposal goes out (14 deals were lost because the review did not finish in time), and give reps a position against Corvid (21 losses).

Method and open questions

All figures come from the five Q3 exports and are in euros. Conversion rates are by deal count. Regional and per-rep attainment is closed-won divided by quota. The win-rate-weighted view of open pipeline multiplies each rep's open pipeline by their Q3 win rate; it is a rough comparison, not a forecast.

The monthly bookings export gives Q3 closed-won as €4,648k; the rep export and the stage flow give €4,600k. The €48k difference is not explained by these files. Loss reasons for earlier quarters and the reason D. Hughes is at 21% of quota (ramping, territory or performance) are not in the data.
