Q3 2026 pipeline review

July–September 2026 · Prepared by Revenue Operations for the quarterly sales review · All values in euros

Q3 closed €4.65M against a €5.10M target (91%), and the shortfall grew each month. Pipeline creation is not the cause: new pipeline reached a 12-month high in September. The loss is at one stage. Only 38% of proposals moved on to negotiation, down from 51–53% in the three quarters before, while every other stage held steady.

1. Bookings fell behind target while pipeline creation grew

2. The leak is at the proposal stage

Of 268 deals that reached proposal in Q3, 102 moved to negotiation, 131 were lost and 35 have sat for more than 60 days. Lost and stalled proposals add up to €7.8M, more than the €4.6M the funnel won. Lead disqualification removes more value in absolute terms, but its rate has not moved in a year.

What the reasons point to

  • Price and budget were found out too late. 47 deals (36%) were lost because the price was above the buyer's approved budget, which is something a proposal should not be the first to discover.
  • Deals are going quiet. 38 were lost to no decision. Add the 35 proposals stalled past 60 days and 73 of the 166 proposals that did not advance simply stopped moving.
  • Two reasons have a specific fix. 21 deals went to Corvid and 14 were lost because the security review did not finish in time.
  • Size of the prize. At Q2's 52% conversion, Q3's proposals would have sent about 37 more deals to negotiation and produced about 26 more wins, roughly €1.2M–€1.7M. The Q3 shortfall was €0.45M.

3. Bookings depend on two reps, and open pipeline sits where win rates are lowest

L. Brandt and A. Okafor closed €2,080k, 45% of the total. The other 7 reps closed 71% of their combined quota, and 3 of 9 reps reached quota. EMEA's 134% is those two reps: the rest of EMEA is at 63%, level with North America's 64%.

What stands out

  • North America holds the most open pipeline and converts the least of it. €4.59M open, with win rates of 15–25% and cycles of 59–71 days. EMEA's top two reps win 47–48% in 36–41 days.
  • D. Hughes is the outlier in EMEA. €100k closed against a €480k quota (21%) at a 14% win rate, with €1.00M still open.
  • Quota adds up to less than the target. The nine reps closed €4.60M against €4.60M of combined quota, which is 100%, in a quarter that missed the €5.10M bookings target by 9%.

4. What to fix in Q4

Recommendations from Revenue Operations, in order of expected effect on Q4 bookings.

  1. Confirm budget before a proposal goes out

    Make an approved budget range a required exit criterion for the Qualified stage, and give reps a lower-priced package or phased option to offer when the budget is short.

    Evidence: 47 of 131 proposal losses were on price above approved budget.

  2. Work the stalled proposals in October and stop new ones from going quiet

    Review all 35 proposals stalled past 60 days (€1.7M) deal by deal in the first two weeks of Q4: re-engage or close out. From now on, send every proposal with an agreed next step and decision date, and flag any proposal with no buyer activity for 30 days.

    Evidence: 38 lost to no decision plus 35 stalled, 44% of the proposals that did not advance.

  3. Start security review at qualification and arm the team against Corvid

    Send the security questionnaire as soon as a deal is qualified so it is not on the critical path at proposal. Run a win-loss review of the 21 Corvid losses and turn it into a comparison sheet for reps.

    Evidence: 14 deals lost to security review timing, 21 to Corvid.

  4. Inspect North America's pipeline before it goes into the Q4 forecast

    €4.59M of open pipeline at a 15–25% win rate should not be counted at face value. Run a deal-by-deal review with the regional manager, remove what is not real, and pair Nguyen, Castillo and Reyes with Brandt or Okafor on how they qualify and run proposals.

    Evidence: 43% of open pipeline, 24% of bookings, 64% of quota, cycles of 59–71 days.

  5. Reduce the dependence on two reps and close the quota gap

    Agree a plan for D. Hughes's €1.00M of open pipeline, and decide how Q4 is covered if Brandt or Okafor has an ordinary quarter. Check whether Q4 quotas add up to the Q4 target; in Q3 they were €0.50M short of it.

    Evidence: 45% of bookings from two reps; the other 7 at 71% of quota.

The measure to watch: proposal-to-negotiation conversion, reviewed monthly, back to 50% or better by the end of Q4.

Notes on the data

  • Loss reasons cover Q3 proposal-stage losses only, with no earlier quarter to compare and no split by region or rep. They show what Q3's lost proposals had in common. They do not show which reason grew, or whether the proposal-stage drop is concentrated in one region.
  • The "size of the prize" range applies Q2's proposal conversion and Q3's negotiation win rate to Q3's proposal count, valued at the average lost-proposal deal size (low end) and the average won deal size (high end). It is an estimate of scale, not a forecast.
  • Monthly bookings for Q3 sum to €4,648k; the by-rep export sums to €4,600k. The €48k difference is not explained by the exports.
  • Combined rep quota is €4,600k against a quarterly bookings target of €5,100k. If other quota carriers or channels exist outside the by-rep export, the gap may be smaller than shown.
  • Stage conversion is counted by deals. Stalled proposals count as not advanced, which is how the 38% in the quarterly export is calculated (102 of 268).
