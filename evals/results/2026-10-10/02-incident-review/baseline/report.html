Checkout outage, 17 September 2026: post-incident review

SEV-1 · Change CHG-4471 · All times UTC · Prepared 10 October 2026 · Customer success: the merchant explanation is in section 5

At 13:52 a configuration change cut the memory limit of the feature cache from 24 GB to 8 GB. The cache emptied, the fraud service that relies on it became about 30 times slower, and 14 minutes later the payments service ran out of connections waiting for it. From 14:06 to 14:53 almost every card checkout failed. Rolling the change back at 14:46 restored service.

How a cache change reached checkout

Checkout depends on Payments, which depends on Fraud, which depends on the cache. Nothing on that path cut a slow call short, so the slowdown passed from one service to the next until checkout stopped.

Why it took 61 minutes to fix

Our only alert was on checkout errors, three systems away from the cause. It fired 17 minutes after the change, and it took another 29 minutes to trace the failure back to the cache.

What we are changing

Payments will stop waiting on a slow Fraud service. Cache configuration changes will roll out gradually and roll back automatically. We will alert on the cache and on fraud scoring directly.

1. How one cache change took down checkout

Every card checkout passes through the same chain of services. The cache sits at the far end of it, three calls downstream of the Checkout service. Requests travel left to right; the failure travelled right to left, in the order 1 to 5.

  1. 1Feature cache With a third of its memory, the cache evicted most of its contents. Its hit rate fell from 97% to 12%.
  2. 2Fraud service Fraud had to recompute features from the feature store on most requests. Scoring time rose from about 45 ms to more than 1.2 s.
  3. 3Payments service Payments has 200 connections to Fraud. Each slow call held one for longer, and at 14:06 all 200 were in use. Nothing cut the slow calls short.
  4. 4Checkout service Payment authorisations stopped completing. Checkout waited about 10 s per request, and errors went from 6.7% to 94% in five minutes.
  5. 5Customers Checkout failed in the web and mobile apps. The orders database, token vault and card processor were working normally throughout.

3. Where the 61 minutes went

From the change to mitigation was 61 minutes. Customers were affected for 47 of them. Nearly half the total, 29 minutes, was spent finding the cause.

13:52 to 14:06Slowing down with no alert
14 min

Cache hit rate and fraud scoring time moved within a minute of the change, but neither had an alert. Checkout errors crept from 0.4% to 6.7% without paging anyone.

14:06 to 14:09Checkout failing, page not yet fired
3 min

Payments ran out of connections and checkout errors passed 30%. First customer reports arrived. The page fired three minutes later and the incident was declared SEV-1.

14:09 to 14:38Looking for the cause
29 min

The page pointed at checkout, and the visible fault was the exhausted connection pool in Payments, so Payments was restarted at 14:21. The pool filled again within 90 seconds. It took a further 17 minutes to follow the path to fraud scoring time and find the cache change.

14:38 to 14:46Rolling the change back
8 min

The cache memory limit was restored to 24 GB.

14:46 to 14:53Cache warming up
7 min

The cache refilled, fraud scoring sped up and Payments connections freed up. Checkout response time dropped from about 10 s to 2.5 s at 14:53.

Change to mitigation: 61 min. The incident was closed at 15:10 once the error rate was back to baseline.

Why we found out late

We paged on one signal, the checkout error rate. That signal is the last thing to move. Fraud scoring time had tripled by 13:53 and was more than 25 times normal by 14:05, and the cache hit rate had already fallen to 12%, but neither was alerted on. For 14 minutes the system was heading for failure and nobody was told.

Why the cause was hard to find

The page said "checkout", and the first broken thing responders saw was the connection pool in Payments. Restarting Payments was a reasonable first move, and it cost time: the fault was two systems further down. Nothing in our alerting connected checkout errors to a cache change made 17 minutes before the page.

5. For customer success: what to tell merchants

A plain-language account you can adapt. Every figure in it comes from this review.

What happened

On 17 September 2026, between 14:06 and 14:53 UTC, most card checkouts on our platform failed. For about 13 minutes before that, from 13:53, checkout was slower than usual and a small share of attempts failed.

The cause was a configuration change we made to an internal system that supports our fraud checks. It made fraud checks very slow, and payments could not complete while waiting for them. It was not caused by your integration or by the card processor.

We reversed the change at 14:46. Checkout recovered over the following minutes and was fully back to normal by 15:10. In total 18,420 checkout attempts failed, affecting 15,890 customers.

What we are doing about it

If our fraud system is slow in future, payments will no longer wait for it. They will fall back to a simpler set of fraud rules, so checkout keeps working.

Changes of this kind will be applied to a small part of the system first and reversed automatically if they cause problems.

We are adding monitoring that warns us at the source of a problem like this, before it reaches checkout, and we will test this failure regularly.

Check before you promise

  • Whether any customer was charged for a checkout that failed. The card processor was working normally, but the incident data does not answer this either way.
  • Figures for an individual merchant. The impact numbers here are platform totals.
  • How the €38,000 in SLA credits is divided between merchants, and when it will be paid.
  • Which of the fixes are already live (see the status column in section 4).

6. Open points

  • Mitigation time. The timeline gives 14:53 as the point the error rate fell below 5%, which is where the 47-minute duration comes from. The per-minute data shows 20.2% at 14:53, with the rate first under 5% at 14:57. Response times did recover sharply at 14:53. If 14:57 is the right mark, the outage was 51 minutes.
  • Action status. Two actions had due dates before this report was prepared: alerting on fraud scoring time and cache hit rate (SRE, 2 October) and the circuit breaker on Payments to Fraud (Payments team, 9 October). Their completion needs confirming.
  • How change CHG-4471 was approved and rolled out. The data records that the change was deployed, not how it was reviewed or why the limit was lowered. That would help the Platform team scope the canary requirement.
