People team Employee engagement survey 2026

Engagement rose to 74, but confidence in career growth fell sharply and is lowest in Engineering and Customer Support

Thirteen of the sixteen questions scored higher than last year. The three that fell are all about career growth, by 8 to 11 points, and only about a third of people in Engineering and Customer Support are favourable on career growth. We propose starting there.

Most scores improved; every question that fell is about career growth

The largest gains are in leadership and workload. “Senior leaders communicate a clear direction” rose 7 points to 71%, and “My workload is manageable” rose 6 points to 63%. Scores for managers were already the highest in the survey and rose again, to 86% and 88%.

Career growth moved the other way. Fewer than half of respondents now see a path to advance (48%, down from 59%) or think promotions are fair (44%, down from 52%). About half (52%, down from 60%) have had a meaningful development conversation in the last six months. These are now the three lowest scores in the survey.

Reading the scores

Favourable means the person agreed or strongly agreed, on a five-point scale. Changes are in percentage points.

On the career growth questions, the people who are not favourable are split between unsure and unhappy: 29% to 31% are neutral on the path to advance and on promotion fairness, and 23% to 25% disagree. Two other scores improved but remain low: “I am paid fairly” at 55% and “Other teams are easy to work with” at 61%.

The low scores sit in three places: career growth in Engineering and Customer Support, workload in Customer Support, tools in Operations

On most themes the eight departments are within a few points of each other. Three results stand well apart from the rest, each in a department of more than 200 respondents.

Engineering and Customer Support are 540 of the 1,284 respondents, 42% of the total. Their career growth scores, 34% and 31%, are 14 to 23 points below every other department. No department scores above 54% on this theme, so it is weak everywhere and much weaker in these two.

Workload improved across the company, but Customer Support is well behind: 49% favourable, against 63% to 69% in every other department. In Operations, 62% say they have the tools they need, against 73% to 81% elsewhere.

Customer Support is the department with the most to fix: it is lowest on career growth, workload and pay and recognition (59%). Managers are a strength everywhere, at 85% to 95%, including in the departments with the lowest scores.

What we propose to do first

The order follows the size of the gap and the number of people affected. The survey shows where scores are low, not why, so each priority starts by asking the people concerned.

For people managers

Manager scores are the highest in the survey. The development conversation is the career growth measure managers control most directly.

First priority

Career growth, starting in Engineering and Customer Support

It is the only theme that fell, it holds the three lowest scores in the survey, and the two weakest departments are 42% of respondents. The three questions point to three things to fix: whether people can see a path, whether promotion decisions are seen as fair, and whether development conversations happen.

For career growth, the first step is listening sessions in Engineering and Customer Support to learn what is behind the scores. Alongside that, check that career paths and promotion criteria exist and are published for every role, and ask every manager to hold a development conversation with each team member: 48% of respondents did not report a meaningful one in the last six months.

What this data cannot tell us

Limits

We cannot say where career growth fell. Department results are for 2026 only. Engineering and Customer Support have the lowest scores now; whether they fell further than others since 2025 is not known from this data.

Theme scores by department are not directly comparable with question scores. Weighting the department career growth scores by respondents gives 42%, while the three career growth questions average 48% company-wide. The theme calculation should be confirmed before the two are quoted side by side. This report compares departments only with each other.

Small departments move easily. In People (44 respondents) or Product and Design (60), one person is about two percentage points. Differences of a few points between the smaller departments should not be read as real.

Nineteen per cent did not respond. 301 of the 1,585 people invited did not answer, and we do not know how their views differ. Last year's response rate is not in the data.

Method

Favourable is agree or strongly agree on a five-point scale. Results for groups of fewer than 10 respondents are suppressed; every department here has at least 44 respondents.
