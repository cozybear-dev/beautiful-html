Employee engagement survey 2026 · Results for the executive team and people managers · People team, October 2026

Engagement is up. Career growth is the one area that got worse.

1,284 of 1,585 people (81%) answered. The engagement index rose from 71 to 74 and eNPS from 19 to 24. Of the 16 questions, 13 improved on last year, led by senior leaders communicating a clear direction (up 7 points) and manageable workload (up 6).

The three that fell are the three questions on career growth, each down 8 to 11 points. Fewer than half of respondents now see a path to advance here (48%) or think promotions are fair (44%).

The problems are concentrated in three places, and we recommend acting on them in this order: career growth in Engineering and Customer Support, workload in Customer Support, and tools in Operations.

What got better and what got worse

Every question, ordered from the biggest fall to the biggest gain.

  • Worse: career growth, on all three questions. Seeing a path to advance fell 11 points to 48%. Having had a meaningful development conversation in the last six months fell 8 to 52%. Believing promotions are fair fell 8 to 44%, the lowest score in the survey.
  • Better: leadership and workload. Senior leaders communicating a clear direction rose 7 points to 71%, trust in their decisions rose 5 to 68%, and manageable workload rose 6 to 63%.
  • Better, but still low. Being paid fairly (55%, up 2) and finding other teams easy to work with (61%, up 3) are the weakest scores outside career growth, and neither moved much.
  • Strong and holding. The two questions about managers remain the highest in the survey, at 86% and 88%.

Changes of 3 points or fewer are small enough that they could be chance rather than a real shift. 5 of the 13 gains are that small; all three falls are well beyond it.

Where the problems are concentrated

Four results stand well apart from the rest, and three of the four are in Engineering and Customer Support.

  • Career growth in Engineering (34%) and Customer Support (31%). The other six departments sit between 48% and 54%. These two departments make up 540 respondents, 42% of everyone who answered. Career growth is the lowest-scoring theme in every department, so this is a company-wide weakness that is much worse in two places.
  • Workload in Customer Support (49%). Every other department is between 63% and 69%. Workload improved across the company this year, which makes Support the exception.
  • Tools in Operations (62%). Every other department is between 73% and 81%.
  • Customer Support is the lowest-scoring department on three themes: career growth, workload, and pay and recognition (59%). Its scores for managers (88%) and purpose (82%) are in line with everyone else.

We do not have department results for 2025, so this shows where scores are lowest now. It cannot show which departments drove the fall in career growth, or whether the workload and tools gaps are new.

What we should do first

These are the People team’s recommendations, for the executive team to decide. The survey shows where the problems are and not why, so each one starts with finding out.

Career growth in Engineering and Customer Support

It is the only area that got worse, it holds the lowest scores in the survey, and these two departments are 42% of respondents. Their managers score 89% and 88%, so the problem is more likely to lie in career paths and the promotion process than in the relationship with the manager.

First steps

  • Run listening sessions in both departments to learn what changed since last year.
  • Ask every manager to hold a development conversation with each person on their team. Only 52% of respondents say they have had one in the last six months, down from 60%, and it is the career question managers can change directly.
  • Review how promotion criteria and decisions are explained. Only 44% think promotions are fair, and that result is for the whole company.

Workload in Customer Support

Fewer than half of Support respondents (49%) are favourable about workload, at least 14 points behind every other department, in a year when the company as a whole improved.

First step

  • Review staffing, shift patterns and contact volumes with Support leadership, and ask the team whether the pressure is the amount of work during hours or being unable to switch off afterwards. This data does not separate the two by department.

Tools in Operations

62% of Operations respondents say they have the tools they need, 11 points behind the next-lowest department. With 284 respondents, Operations is the second-largest department.

First step

  • Ask Operations teams which tools are holding them back. The survey has one question on tools and does not name them.

Keep watching

  • Fair pay, at 55% (up 2). One in five respondents is unfavourable.
  • Working with other teams, at 61% (up 3), against 85% for collaboration inside teams.

Protect

  • Manager scores of 85% or higher in every department.
  • The gains in leadership communication and trust, both now around 70%.

How to read these results

  • The survey ran from 1 to 19 September 2026. Favourable = agree or strongly agree on a five-point scale. Results for groups under 10 respondents are suppressed. The smallest department here has 44 respondents, so nothing is suppressed in this report.
  • eNPS is the employee Net Promoter Score, which runs from −100 to +100.
  • Small departments move more on a handful of answers. For People (44 respondents), Product and Design (60) and Finance and Legal (72), a gap of several points from other departments is not meaningful on its own. The four results highlighted above are in departments of 228 to 312 respondents and are at least 11 points behind every department that is not highlighted.
  • The “3 points or fewer” guide for year-on-year changes assumes a similar number of people answered in 2025.
  • Department results are available by theme only, for 2026 only. Theme scores by department and question scores for the whole company are calculated separately and do not average to exactly the same figure, so this report uses question scores for the company picture and department scores only to compare departments with each other.
