Only the segment that got the new pricing improved

In April we replaced per-seat pricing with usage bands for enterprise customers. Their churn fell in the first full month and has stayed down. Mid-market and small-business customers kept per-seat pricing, and their churn in September is where it was in January.

How churn is measured

Customers lost in the month, as a share of customers at the start of the month. A downgrade is not counted as a loss.

Nothing else changed for enterprise customers in the period: the product releases, the support model and the account team were the same for all three segments. That makes the other two segments a fair comparison.

Accounts that moved to usage bands almost never left

Not every enterprise account moved. Of the 530, 412 took the new plan at their renewal and 118 stayed on per-seat pricing. Between April and September, 8 of the 412 left. Of the 118 that stayed on the old plan, 14 left: six times the rate.

A caution

Accounts chose whether to move, so those that moved may have been the more committed ones. The comparison with other segments in Figure 1 does not have this problem.

The new price is worked out where usage is already measured

No new system was needed. The metering service already recorded usage for reporting; billing now reads each account's band from it once a month. Extending the scheme to another segment is a change of configuration, not of architecture.

Mid-market is the next place to try it

Mid-market customers look most like enterprise customers in how their usage varies from month to month, which is what per-seat pricing punishes. Small businesses use the product steadily and would gain little.

Recommendation

Offer usage bands to mid-market customers at renewal from January

If mid-market responds half as well as enterprise did, churn falls from 2.5% to about 1.8% a month, which keeps roughly 160 customers a year.

Offer at renewal

Lowest risk. Reaches all mid-market accounts within twelve months. This is what we did for enterprise.

Move everyone in January

Fastest, but a third of accounts would pay more in their first month, and we would learn nothing from a comparison.

Wait another quarter

More certainty about enterprise, at the cost of about 40 mid-market customers.

Method

Churn is logo churn: accounts cancelled in the month divided by accounts active on the first day. Revenue kept is the difference between September and January enterprise churn, applied to the average enterprise contract of 20,100 euros a year.
