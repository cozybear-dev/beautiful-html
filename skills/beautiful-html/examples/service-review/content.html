The night shift cut the wait for a critical alert by three quarters

Until May, an alert that fired after 18:00 waited for the morning. Since then an analyst is on shift around the clock, and the median wait has fallen every month. High-severity alerts followed the same curve at a slower pace, because critical ones are read first.

How we count

From the moment an alert is raised to the moment an analyst opens it. Median of all alerts of that severity in the month.

Nine in ten alerts close without a person, and four in ten of the rest arrive after hours

The platform raised 10,643 alerts in the quarter. Automation closed 9,350 of them. Analysts read the other 1,293 and escalated nine to your team.

Coverage rose everywhere except the warehouse control network

A system without a sensor cannot raise an alert. Cloud workloads gained most this quarter, after the deployment was added to the build pipeline. The warehouse control network did not move: 22 of its 38 systems run software the sensor does not support.

Two incidents, both contained the same night

Nine alerts were escalated. Seven were confirmed as harmless within the hour. Two were incidents.

Three ways to watch the warehouse network

Passive network sensor

Listens at the two switches that carry all control traffic. Covers all 38 systems, changes none of them. Four weeks.

Upgrade the 22 systems

Makes them fit for the standard sensor. Needs two maintenance windows per warehouse; realistic by June.

Logs from the jump host only

A week of work. Shows who connected, not what they did.

Recommendation

Install the passive sensor in November, then upgrade at the pace the warehouses allow

It is the only option that covers every control system this year without touching one.

Method

An alert counts as read when an analyst opens it. Coverage is the share of systems in the asset inventory whose sensor checked in during the last seven days of the quarter. Times are UTC.
