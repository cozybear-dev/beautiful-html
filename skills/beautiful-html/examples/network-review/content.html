Two routes reach the warehouse controls unchecked

Prepared by Hunt & Hackett for the head of infrastructure, Meridian Freight. 14 October 2026.

Nine of twelve flows pass a control; three do not

Every managed route into the warehouse network goes through one recorded door, the jump host. The legacy VPN and one engineering workstation go around it, and the VPN also opens the file servers.

We listed every flow that crosses a zone boundary and asked one question of each: what checks who is connecting? For nine, the answer is the access broker, the jump host or a fixed service account. For three, it is a shared password or nothing.

The firewall allows far more than the flows need

The three red routes exist because the rules permit them. Between office and operations, 61 of every 100 rules allow any port. Rewriting the rules around the nine managed flows closes most of what is open and breaks nothing we observed in September.

61 of 100 rules allow any port

Three changes, in the order we would make them

  1. Move both suppliers to the access broker

    Then switch the legacy VPN off. Removes three of the three red routes' starting point. Six weeks.

  2. Put the engineering tool on the jump host

    The vendor supports it. Sessions to the controllers are then recorded like every other. Two weeks.

  3. Replace the "any" rules with the nine flows

    Run the new rules in log-only mode for a month first, so nothing breaks unseen.

Method

Flows are from firewall and switch records for 1 to 30 September 2026, grouped by source and destination system. A "session" is one connection from start to end. Open paths were counted from the rule set, not from traffic, so they include paths nobody used.
